# Week 2 — Business Analyst Interview Preparation

These are concise speaking drafts based on known resume evidence. Maris should practise aloud and correct any detail that does not match personal memory. Do not memorise them word-for-word.

## 20 behavioural questions with STAR/CAR answer drafts

### 1. Tell me about yourself

I am a software-engineering postgraduate with technical business-analysis experience. At DT Produce I documented BPMN processes and helped digitise accounting, payroll and agricultural workflows; at Camera House I built Python automation that reduced price-tag preparation time by about 90%. I am now strengthening formal requirements, traceability and UAT practice for a Technical BA role bridging operational and technical stakeholders.

### 2. Tell me about a process you improved

Context: Camera House generated bulk price tags through a manual Excel-to-Photoshop workflow. Action: I analysed the repeated steps and created a Python application to automate generation. Result: preparation time fell by approximately 90%, showing the value of understanding the process before selecting technology.

### 3. Describe a digital transformation example

Context: DT Produce relied on traditional, partly paper-based agricultural and administration processes after COVID disruption. Action: I analysed workflows, documented requirements and contributed to OCR, timesheet and Angular solutions. Result: data entry and payroll errors were reduced and business processes became more standardised.

### 4. Describe a challenging requirement

Context: The DT salary process combined timesheet capture with payroll data needs. Action: I clarified fields and workflow, documented the process and developed a Flutter application integrated with Google Sheets. Result: direct synchronisation reduced manual payroll-processing errors. In an interview I would add specific constraints only from records I can verify.

### 5. How do you handle ambiguity?

I make assumptions visible, identify the decision owner and use a small model or prototype to create concrete discussion. In CareerTrack NT, I separated a basic follow-up date from automated reminders because identity, consent, channels and timezones were unresolved. That kept the core story deliverable without hiding important uncertainty.

### 6. Tell me about working with a technical delivery team

In my current internship I collaborate with engineering leads to break down technical requirements, test assumptions and improve application logic. My technical background helps me discuss feasibility, while the BA discipline reminds me to preserve the business outcome and acceptance criteria rather than jump directly to a solution.

### 7. Tell me about teamwork

At DT Produce I worked in an Agile team of six contributing Angular modules and related analysis. I supported shared understanding through requirements and process documentation, then worked with the implementation rather than treating documentation as a handoff. The experience reinforced that questions and decisions need to remain visible to the whole team.

### 8. How do you manage competing priorities?

I compare business value, risk, dependency and deadline, then make the trade-off explicit. For CareerTrack NT, create/list/update are Must items, summary is Should and reminders are Could because reminders introduce more security and operational dependencies. I would confirm that order with the product decision-maker.

### 9. Tell me about a mistake or failed assumption

My approach is to surface assumptions early and test them cheaply. A safe example is treating a prototype as evidence: a screen that looks correct does not prove the underlying rule works. I now link rules to negative tests and would discuss a more specific workplace example only if I can accurately reconstruct its context, action and result.

### 10. How do you handle stakeholder conflict?

I separate positions from underlying needs, define the decision criteria and show the impact of each option. I would document areas of agreement, unresolved choices and the accountable decision-maker. This avoids forcing false consensus and gives the delivery team a traceable decision.

### 11. Describe communicating technical information

At Camera House I explain cameras, lenses and digital-imaging equipment to customers with different technical knowledge. I focus first on the person's intended outcome, then translate specifications into practical consequences. The same technique helps a BA explain APIs or data constraints without unnecessary jargon.

### 12. How do you ensure requirement quality?

I check that each requirement is necessary, unambiguous, feasible, atomic, consistent and testable. I add business rules and examples, link it to a source and acceptance test, and ask delivery and quality stakeholders to challenge it. CareerTrack NT date rules show how a vague “valid date” requirement becomes observable.

### 13. Describe learning a new tool quickly

My work has required moving among Python, Flutter, Angular, Google APIs and process modelling. I start with the smallest useful outcome, follow authoritative material and create a working example. I document what I can demonstrate and keep planned tools separate from completed skills.

### 14. How do you respond to feedback?

I restate the feedback as a change or question, check the underlying objective and update the relevant artefact rather than only the presentation. If a validation rule changes, I update the rule, story, API contract and test traceability together. This makes feedback actionable and prevents inconsistent documents.

### 15. Describe working under pressure

I reduce risk by clarifying the minimum valuable outcome, dependencies and verification steps. I avoid hiding scope or skipping validation because rushed defects cause more delay. I communicate what is done, what is at risk and what decision is needed.

### 16. How do you facilitate a workshop?

I define the objective, participants, pre-reading and decisions required. During the session I timebox discussion, use a visible process/story model and capture decisions, assumptions and owners. Afterwards I circulate the output for confirmation rather than assuming silence means approval.

### 17. Tell me about using data in a decision

The Camera House automation provided a measurable efficiency result: about a 90% reduction in tag-creation time. That result is stronger than saying the process “felt faster.” As a BA, I look for a baseline and outcome measure that connects a solution to business value.

### 18. What motivates you?

I enjoy making an operational problem understandable and then seeing a practical solution improve the workflow. My combined BA/development background is most useful when I can connect stakeholder language, process logic and implementable detail.

### 19. How do you handle sensitive information?

I minimise collection, restrict access and avoid placing real personal data in demonstrations or shared repositories. CareerTrack NT deliberately excludes resumes, credentials and public deployment until identity and privacy controls are designed. I also avoid logging sensitive values or exposing internal error details.

### 20. Why should we hire you for a Technical BA role?

I bring hands-on process analysis plus enough development experience to communicate credibly with delivery teams. I can point to BPMN and requirements work at DT Produce, measurable Python automation at Camera House and current full-stack collaboration. I am also honest about learning gaps and turn them into demonstrable plans rather than overstating seniority.

## 20 technical BA questions and concise answers

1. **What is a business requirement?** A high-level outcome the organisation needs; it should not prematurely prescribe a design.
2. **Functional vs non-functional requirement?** Functional describes behaviour; non-functional constrains qualities such as security, accessibility or performance.
3. **What makes a good requirement?** Necessary, clear, feasible, consistent, traceable, prioritised and testable.
4. **What is BPMN?** A standard notation for processes using events, activities, gateways and flows.
5. **Current-state vs future-state?** Current-state explains how work operates now; future-state models the intended process after change.
6. **What is a user story?** A concise statement of user, need and value that invites conversation and is completed by acceptance criteria.
7. **Acceptance criteria vs Definition of Done?** Criteria are story-specific outcomes; Definition of Done is the recurring quality bar for every item.
8. **What is traceability?** The maintained relationship among need, requirement, design, implementation and test evidence.
9. **Verification vs validation?** Verification asks whether the artefact is well formed; validation asks whether it supports the real need.
10. **What is UAT?** Representative business users confirm the solution supports agreed business scenarios.
11. **How do you prioritise?** Use value, risk, urgency and dependency; MoSCoW is one transparent technique.
12. **What happens in refinement?** Clarify, split, estimate, reorder and add testable detail to upcoming backlog items.
13. **What is scope creep?** Uncontrolled expansion without assessing value, impact, priority and approval.
14. **What is a use case?** A structured actor/system interaction including main and alternative flows.
15. **Why does a BA need SQL?** To investigate data, validate rules/results and communicate precisely about information needs.
16. **What is an API?** A defined interface that lets software components exchange requests and responses.
17. **How would you analyse an integration?** Define systems of record, contract, mapping, validation, errors, security, volume and ownership.
18. **How do wireframes help?** They expose information and interaction assumptions cheaply before implementation.
19. **What is stakeholder analysis?** Identifying affected parties, interests, influence, impact and suitable engagement.
20. **How would you handle a changed requirement?** Record the change and rationale, trace affected artefacts/tests, assess value/risk/cost, obtain the required decision and communicate the approved outcome.

## Questions to ask an interviewer

- Which decisions is the BA accountable for versus facilitating?
- How are requirements, designs and test evidence traced today?
- What is the balance of process, data, integration and UX analysis?
- What does successful delivery in the first three months look like?
