Strong findings from the combined analysis
Bore purpose and yield
    Monitoring bores have the highest average yield: 4.89 L/s.
    Production bores average 3.59 L/s.
    Investigation bores average 2.94 L/s.
    Unknown-purpose bores have the lowest average: 1.93 L/s.
Risk class and yield
    Risk Class 1 has the highest average yield: 4.89 L/s.
    Risk Class 3 averages only 1.64 L/s.
    Risk Class 4 has the lowest average at 1.18 L/s.
    This gives us a potentially interesting relationship to visualise.
Current vs historical
    Current bores average 3.80 L/s.
    Historical bores average 1.41 L/s.
    That's a substantial difference and is probably one of your strongest findings.
Water-quality coverage
    Current bores have 79,797 water-quality samples.
    Historical bores have 5,210.
    This is useful for showing that water-quality monitoring is much more concentrated around current bores.
High-yield + water-quality relationship
    We can see examples such as RN007929 with 256 L/s, TDS 490, EC 690, pH 8.2.
    RN006467 has 125 L/s but substantially higher TDS/EC values.
    This gives us a good opportunity to investigate whether high-yield bores necessarily have better water quality.
Monitoring stations
    The groundwater monitoring table successfully joins to Bores through UFI.
    This confirms the relational design is useful for combining monitoring information with bore characteristics.
